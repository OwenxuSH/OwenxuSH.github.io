About

About me.

I study how to formulate scientific problems so that AI outputs can become useful, testable evidence.

Curiosity across disciplines.

I study Computer Science and Technology at ShanghaiTech University. My research interests center on AI for Science, especially computational biology, multimodal learning, generative world models, and ways to test whether model outputs support meaningful claims.

My projects span wearable motion sensing, animal behavior, context-specific genetic interactions, cancer drug response, and video generation. The domains differ, but each has led me to ask a similar question: what must a model represent, and what evidence would show that it has learned something useful?

Research philosophy

Choose the question before optimizing the answer.

Modern AI is becoming remarkably good at optimization. Once a task has been reduced to a dataset, a metric, and an objective, implementation and iterative improvement can increasingly be accelerated by coding agents. That makes the upstream choices more consequential: is this the right objective, and what would count as evidence?

A drug-response model can lower prediction error while offering explanations that are hard to validate. A behavior model can score well while using a representation that misses meaningful behaviors. A video generator can make a convincing future without modeling the causal process that produced it.

I work backward from the scientific claim. I ask what capability a system should have, what evidence would support that claim, and what current benchmarks may be hiding. The answer may call for a new model, a better representation, a more informative benchmark, or a stronger validation method.

That is how I think about AI for Science: formulating scientific questions into computational problems that are worth optimizing.

Looking forward

From prediction toward intervention, mechanism, and discovery.

In biology, I want to study models that help us reason about what will change, why it changes, and how it might be deliberately altered.

In generative world models, I want to move from plausibility toward structured dynamics and causality—understanding that can be tested under intervention rather than assumed from appearance.

AI is becoming increasingly capable of optimizing the objectives we specify. Science still requires us to decide which objectives are worth optimizing.
