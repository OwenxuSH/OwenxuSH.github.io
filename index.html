The question behind my work

What must a model capture before its output becomes scientifically meaningful?

As coding agents make well-specified objectives increasingly easy to optimize, I believe the scarce part of research is shifting upstream: choosing the right objective, representation, and evidence in the first place.

A drug-response model may achieve lower error while offering explanations that cannot be validated. A behavior-recognition system may achieve high accuracy while operating in the wrong representational space. A video generator may produce visually convincing futures while failing to model the causal process that produced them.

These are often the real research problems. I work backward from the scientific claim: what capability should a system possess, what evidence would support it, and what failure might a benchmark be hiding?

01 / AI for Biology

Represent biology in a way that supports discovery.

I am interested in AI systems that model biology while preserving the context, heterogeneity, and structure that make predictions meaningful. My projects range from context-specific synthetic lethality and cancer drug response to open-vocabulary animal behavior.

Across them, I want representations and explanations that can support intervention, mechanism, and discovery.

02 / Generative world models

Test whether plausible outputs reflect real dynamics.

I ask whether generative models can move from producing plausible observations to representing how the world actually evolves. A useful world model should preserve entities and states, respect event dependencies, and respond selectively to interventions.

My work on CausalCascade tests this gap in image-to-video generation.

Selected work

Four projects, one evolving question.

Each project exposed a different gap between model output and scientific understanding.

01 / Context

Context-specific synthetic lethality

The same gene pair can behave differently across cancer cell lines. Our work explored biological representations for both within-cell-line prediction and generalization to unseen cellular contexts.

A prediction is only as meaningful as the context represented by the model.

↗
02 / Representation

MouseGPT

Open-vocabulary descriptions of mouse video make behavior available for semantic profiling, clustering, and novel-behavior discovery, beyond a fixed list of labels or purely kinematic similarity.

Representation determines what a model is capable of discovering.

↗
03 / Evidence

HappyRabbit

Accurate drug-response prediction alone cannot establish whether an explanation is biologically defensible. We paired controlled benchmarking with attribution validation, virtual perturbation, pathway analysis, and literature auditing.

The evidence behind a prediction deserves its own evaluation.

↗
04 / Causality

CausalCascade

Visually plausible video can still miss the events and dependencies that make a physical process coherent. Simulator-grounded scenes and interventions reveal the gap between predicting what happens and generating how it happens.

A plausible future is not necessarily a causal future.

A common thread

What are we actually asking the model to learn, and how would we know if it learned it?

Synthetic lethality asks how biological interactions change with cellular context. MouseGPT asks whether fixed labels can capture the behaviors researchers need to discover. HappyRabbit asks what evidence should accompany a drug-response prediction. CausalCascade asks whether visual plausibility is enough to claim a learned world model.

This is the question I want to keep pursuing: how to make AI useful by identifying the scientific bottleneck first, then building the model and evaluation that can address it.
