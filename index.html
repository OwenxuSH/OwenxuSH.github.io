AI for Science · ShanghaiTech University

Building AI for scientific discovery.

I’m Zhengning Xu, a third-year undergraduate majoring in Computer Science and Technology at ShanghaiTech University. I have conducted research at ShanghaiTech University’s School of Information Science and Technology and is now a research intern at Shanghai Jiao Tong University’s School of Artificial Intelligence (SAI). My current research interest is AI for Science (AI4S).

As AI makes model development and improvement increasingly accessible, I am drawn to the harder question of which scientific problems are worth asking. Across my work, I explore what it takes for AI to become a reliable instrument for science discovery: understanding when biological findings generalize beyond context, turning complex behavior into interpretable descriptions, grounding biomedical predictions in architecture design and solid evidence, and testing whether models capture the causal dynamics in physical worlds.

01 / AI for Biology

From genetic interactions to animal behavior.

I have studied context-specific synthetic lethality, multimodal cell-drug interaction prediction and mechanism explanation, and conducted VLM-based open-vocabulary analysis of animal behavior.

These projects have given me experience with biological data integration, model design, rigorous comparison, and multimodal evaluation.

02 / Sensing & Modeling the Physical World

From human movement to causal event chains.

My work examines physical processes through both measured signals and generated events. In wearable sensing, I analyzed human movement from wristband signals using Dynamic Time Warping; that work was accepted at IEEE ROBIO 2025.

CausalCascade tests whether nine video generation models can produce causally coherent event chains across 50 simulator-grounded physical scenes and paired interventions.
